11 November 2026New reporting requirement

Circular No. 24/2026 introduces monthly social insurance contribution declaration

Issued by the Ministry of Human Resources · Reference MoHR/SI/2026/124

The Ministry of Human Resources has issued Circular No. 24/2026 under Article 22 of the Social Insurance Act No. 7/2026, introducing a new mandatory electronic monthly declaration of insured earnings and social insurance contributions for registered private-sector employers. The reporting obligation takes effect on 1 December 2026.

Change at a glance

Legal instrument
Circular No. 24/2026
Legal basis
Social Insurance Act No. 7/2026, Article 22 — Employer declarations
Effective date
1 December 2026
Affected employers
All registered private-sector employers with covered employees
New obligation
Monthly electronic declaration of insured earnings, employer contributions and employee deductions
Submission deadline
10th calendar day of the month following the reporting month

New statutory reporting duty

Starting with the December 2026 reporting period, employers must electronically submit a monthly contribution declaration through the National Social Insurance Portal. Each declaration must include the employer registration identifier, employee insurance identifiers, monthly insured earnings, employer contribution amounts and employee contribution amounts withheld from payroll.

The declaration for December 2026 must be submitted by 10 January 2027. Employers must correct any rejected declarations and retain confirmation receipts and supporting payroll records for inspection.

Payroll and HR reporting impact

Employers and payroll service providers must prepare the new report from payroll results, reconcile declared amounts to payroll contribution records and establish monthly submission controls. This is a new filing and reporting requirement, not a new contribution calculation rate.

Separate legal development from the contribution-rate decree

This circular establishes a new monthly reporting obligation under Article 22. It does not amend the contribution percentages introduced under Royal Decree No. 18/2026 and Article 14. Both instruments relate to the Social Insurance Act No. 7/2026 but impose different employer obligations.
